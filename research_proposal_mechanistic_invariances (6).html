Mechanistic Interpretability × RL Safety · Research Proposal 2026

Circuit-Level
Detection, Correction
and Repair of RL Failures

Activation monitoring is blind to goal-visible routing failures. The causal graph is not. We demonstrate this empirically — and show that fixing the circuit and fixing the behavior are different problems that require different tools.

Core Claim: RL goal misgeneralization can occur as a routing failure — the agent perceives the goal correctly but its decision circuit has stopped routing through that perception. Standard activation monitoring is blind to this. We show that a W-matrix causal graph (gradient-free, validated r = 0.89) detects routing failures that activation monitoring cannot (k_activation = undefined, k_graph = 200 at the blind spot), that circuit repair and behavioral correction dissociate, and that a routing-targeted reward signal (R_reason, λ = 0.1) corrects the failure where representation-targeted interventions cannot — online, in ~8k steps, without retraining. Empirically demonstrated across four experiments and six reviewer stress-tests on a 624k-parameter PPO policy.

§ 01

The Problem

RL agents are evaluated on what they do — never on how they reason. An agent can achieve high reward during training via spurious internal routing — correlations that happened to work on the training distribution but are not causally grounded in the task. At deployment, when the environment shifts, the routing collapses. No existing method detects this before it happens.

The behavioural framing of each failure mode is established in the literature — Langosco et al. (ICML 2022) for goal misgeneralization, Skalse et al. (NeurIPS 2022) for reward hacking, Hubinger et al. (2019) for internal goal divergence. The unifying mechanistic claim — that all four share a common root in causally miscalibrated internal routing — is the contribution of this research, operationalised and empirically tested here.

The Silent Failure Chain — Shared Root
① Agent trains on an environment — actions look correct, reward is high
② Internally routes through causally invalid pathways — proxy features, shortcuts, spurious correlations that co-occurred with reward during training
③ Environment shifts at deployment — the spurious routing no longer works, or the gap between reward proxy and true goal is exposed
④ Silent failure — no process-level signal fired before the cliff — until now
The Four Failure Modes — One Mechanistic Root
Failure Mode A · Langosco et al. ICML 2022
Goal Misgeneralization
Capability generalises, goal does not. Agent learned to navigate to a proxy location, not to the goal itself. Behaves correctly during training. Fails silently when the goal moves.
Caught by I3 + I4 · Empirically confirmed
Failure Mode B · Skalse et al. NeurIPS 2022
Reward Hacking
Agent finds a shortcut to the reward signal that bypasses genuine task reasoning. Reward is high. The actual objective is not achieved. Can occur on the training distribution — no shift required.
Caught by I1 + I2 · W-based I2 k=+15k confirmed
Failure Mode C · Hubinger et al. 2019
World Model Exploitation
Model-based agent finds strategies that score highly in its internal world model but fail in the real environment. The model's approximation errors become exploitable gaps.
Caught by I2 + I5 · Future work
Failure Mode D · Krakovna et al. 2020 (60+ instances)
Specification Gaming
Agent satisfies the literal reward specification through a causally wrong reasoning chain. Correct output, wrong process. Guaranteed to fail under any non-trivial distribution shift.
Caught by I1 + I2 + I5 · Future work
The behavioural existence of each failure mode is established in prior work. The unifying mechanistic claim — that all four share a common root in causally miscalibrated internal routing, detectable before behavioral failure — is the contribution of this research. Goal misgeneralization and reward hacking are empirically confirmed. World model exploitation and specification gaming are future work.

§ 04

What Solving This Enables

The Reframe
Prior safety methods ask: "did the agent do something bad?" — outcome-level, after the fact. This method asks: "is the agent's causal routing intact?" — process-level, before the fact. Empirically: at the (6,5) blind spot the goal was visible the entire episode, the goal feature was active, yet activation monitoring reported nothing wrong. The causal graph fired at step 0. There is no behavioral signal to wait for — the routing failure is invisible to outcome-level monitoring.
01 · Early Warning
Before behavioral failure — across all four failure modes
All four failure modes produce circuit miscalibration before wrong actions. Detect the cause, not the symptom.
02 · Mechanistic Diagnosis
Which failure mode, why, and where
I1 → shortcut. I3+I4 → goal drift. I2+I5 → world model. Pattern identifies the failure type automatically (Q3: 100% classifier accuracy).
03 · Behavioral Correction
Prevents failure without retraining
R_reason fires k steps early and feeds back as a dense per-step signal. PPO updates the action distribution away from violation-triggering states. Empirically demonstrated: 100% → 0% failure at a routing blind spot, online in ~8k steps without retraining. Requires genuine goal representation (r > 0.3) and λ in [0.10, 0.15].
04 · Durable Repair
Circuit-level fix between episodes
Violation records guide targeted fine-tuning or activation steering. Key empirical finding: these are not interchangeable. Fine-tuning repaired circuit metrics (repaired=True) but left failure at 100% — because the problem was in routing, not representation. Activation steering fixes representation failures (2/3 cases, confirmed Q3). Circuit repair and behavioral correction dissociate.

§ 07

The Core Idea — A Three-Layer Safety System

The Central Insight
Every RL deployment failure — goal misgeneralization, reward hacking, world model exploitation, specification gaming — shares one root: the agent's internal reasoning circuit has become causally miscalibrated. We do not monitor what the agent does. We monitor how it reasons.
What existing methods do
Watch the reward curve. Wait for actions to go wrong. Detect failure after it has already happened.
What we do
Read the causal circuit inside the network. Detect when reasoning has broken — before wrong actions occur. Correct it.
Empirically confirmed on goal misgeneralization (routing failure): k_activation = nan — activation monitoring blind. k_graph = 200 — causal graph fires at step 0. Reward hacking: W-based I2 leads by +15k steps. Experiment 4, 3 seeds.
What We Are Doing — Three Steps
Step 1 — Extract
Build the causal circuit
Train Top-K SAE on the frozen policy. Compute W = DT · WencT — inter-feature causal graph, validated r = 0.89. Extract five invariant profiles. Freeze as G*.
Step 2 — Monitor
Compare G_live vs G* every step
At deployment, read G_live from the live forward pass. Check five invariances. If violated — emit R_reason as a dense per-step penalty before failure manifests.
Step 3 — Repair
Fix the circuit drift durably
Violation records diagnose which features drifted. Route to correct repair: R_reason for routing failures, steering for representation failures. Automated at 100% accuracy (Q3).
The Goals
1
Detect routing failures before behavioral failure
Failures where the goal is visible but ignored — invisible to activation monitoring, detectable by causal graph. k_activation = nan, k_graph = 200.
2
Correct the failure without retraining
R_reason fires k steps early. PPO corrects routing online. 100% → 0% failure at λ=0.1, ~8k steps. No retraining. No environment reset.
3
Automate failure type diagnosis
Routing vs representation failure requires different responses. 100% classifier accuracy routing to the correct one (Q3). No human intervention needed.
4
Generalise across failure modes
Goal misgeneralization confirmed (4 experiments). Reward hacking: W-based I2 leads by k = +15k steps. World model exploitation + specification gaming: future work.
What We Allow — Agent Can Adapt
✓New features become relevant as environment changes
✓Valid training-time edges lose weight — allowed
✓Reasoning depth changes across state types
What We Penalise — Invariance Violations
✗Goal feature causal edge collapses — routing failure (I3)
✗Spurious edges gain weight — reward hacking (I2)
✗Claimed cause does not drive action under intervention (I5)
✗Reasoning chain collapses to depth-1 shortcut (I1)
§ 08

Methodology

Full Pipeline — Training to Deployment
Phase 1 — Extract Causal Circuits During Training
1
Train PPO on MiniGrid until convergence. Freeze all weights. Train Top-K SAE (K=32) on frozen activations — extracting monosemantic features.
2
Apply full activation patching offline. Zero out feature i, measure change in feature j. Valid edges survive — spurious edges flagged. Compute W = D^T · W_enc^T (validated r = 0.89 vs patching).
3
Build G*. Nodes = SAE features. Edges = causally validated influences. Aggregate into reference invariant profiles.
4
Extract five invariant profiles — I1 depth, I2 spurious edges, I3 goal features, I4 proxy ceilings, I5 self-consistency. Freeze as reference. Validate W-matrix vs patching (r = 0.89 confirmed).
Causal Circuit — What G_t Looks Like Inside The Agent
SAE extracts features at each layer. Circuit analysis builds directed causal edges between them. Edge weight encodes causal strength validated by do-calculus intervention.
The Five Causal Invariances — click to expand
Each invariance is independently motivated by causal theory, independently testable, and independently robust to distribution shift.
I1
Invariance 1
Causal Depth
Reasoning chain must have sufficient intermediate steps between input features and action. A shallow input→action direct connection is a shortcut in any environment — it means the agent is pattern-matching, not reasoning.
Bidirectional check
I2
Invariance 2
Spurious Edge Absence
Edges that failed intervention testing during training — known-invalid pathways — must not gain weight at deployment. Valid edges losing weight is allowed. Only the re-entry of pre-identified bad pathways is penalized.
One-directional — blocks bad entry only
I3
Invariance 3
Goal Feature Dependence
Features causally linked to the true reward signal must remain somewhere in the reasoning chain. If the agent stops routing through true goal features entirely, it has undergone goal misgeneralization — regardless of what new path it found.
Presence check — path may change
I4
Invariance 4
Proxy Feature Independence
Features that correlated with reward during training but failed causal validation must not become dominant drivers at deployment. Their causal weight must stay below training-time baselines.
Weight ceiling check
I5
Invariance 5
Causal Self-Consistency
The feature the agent's graph identifies as the dominant cause must actually drive the action under intervention. If zeroing that feature does not change the action, the graph is self-inconsistent. Requires no reference to training — distribution-shift robust by construction.
Live intervention — no training baseline needed
Spotting Violations At Deployment — G_live vs G*
After distribution shift, the agent's live circuit diverges from the frozen reference. Each failure mode produces a distinct structural signature mapped to a specific invariance violation.
§ 08b · The Complete System
Three layers working together: extract offline, monitor live, repair durably
Layer 1 · Offline
Circuit Extraction
Build G* once, offline, using full activation patching. High fidelity, no time pressure. Freeze as the reference invariant profile.
Layer 2 · Real-Time
Monitor + Behavioral Correction
EAP at every step. Compare G_live against G*. Emit R_reason. PPO updates action distribution away from violation-triggering states. The safety net — prevents failure without fixing the circuit.
Layer 3 · Between Episodes
Targeted Repair
Violation records from deployment guide a targeted fine-tuning objective or activation steering vector. Addresses the specific feature drift directly. The cure, not just the bandage.
Layer 2 — Real-Time Monitor + Behavioral Correction
A
Forward pass → frozen SAE → live feature activation vector.
B
Single backward pass over K active features computes the K×K Jacobian. EAP edge weights wliveij = |fi · ∂fj/∂fi| built in microseconds.
C
I1–I4 check G_live against frozen G* profiles — fast matrix comparison. I5 runs one counterfactual forward pass only when I1–I4 flag a violation.
D
R_reason emitted. PPO update makes violation-triggering actions less probable. Agent behaviorally corrects — avoids states and actions that produce miscalibrated circuits.
What Behavioral Correction Actually Does

R_reason does not directly fix the causal circuit. It makes the agent less likely to take the actions that led to circuit miscalibration. This is the safety net — the agent averts the failure by changing its behavior, not by repairing its representations. That is enough to prevent the failure from occurring. The circuit repair happens in Layer 3.

The Reward Objective
Rtotal = Renv + λ(t) · Rreason
where
Rreason = −(α · Vdrop + β · Vgain + γ · 𝟙[I5])
Vdrop catches goal feature collapse (I3+I4). Vgain catches spurious edge re-entry (I2). I5 fires on self-inconsistency. λ(t) decays as agent proves stability.
The Violation Score — Comparing G_live Against G*
Weight Collapse — I3 + I4
Vdrop = Σ max(0, w*ij − wliveij)
Goal edge loses weight. Catches goal feature dropout and proxy dominance.
Edge Re-Entry — I2
Vgain = Σ max(0, wliveij − w*ij)
Spurious edge gains weight. Catches reward hacking. W-based I2: k = +15k steps early warning confirmed.
Vtotal = α · Vdrop + β · Vgain + γ · 𝟙[I5 fires]
Rreason = − Vtotal · λ(t)  ·  λ ∈ [0.10, 0.15] (empirically validated)
What Violation Records Accumulate
→ Which invariances fired at which steps
→ Which features drifted and in which direction
→ How far each edge weight moved from G*
→ The full causal diagnosis — passed to Layer 3
Layer 3 — Targeted Repair (Between Episodes)
Option A — Targeted Fine-Tuning
Between-episode weight repair
Violation records identify exactly which features drifted and which edges changed. Construct a fine-tuning loss that directly penalizes the observed drift. Run a small number of gradient steps. Redeploy with repaired weights.
Durable repair · weight-level fix · no inference cost
Option B — Activation Steering
Mid-episode representation repair
When I5 fires and the circuit identifies that avoid_signal weight collapsed, directly inject a steering vector at the relevant layer to boost that feature's activation. Correction happens at the source — the feature weight — bypassing the gradient path entirely.
Mid-episode · activation-level · no fine-tuning needed
Why Behavioral Correction + Targeted Repair Together

Layer 2 is the traction control — it prevents the wheels from spinning on the ice without fixing the ice. Layer 3 is the repair — it fixes the underlying condition so the agent no longer needs the safety net. Together they form the first complete deployment-time safety system for MBRL agents: detect early, correct behaviorally, repair durably. No prior work offers all three layers.

§ 11

The Experiments — What We Built and What We Found

Four experiments, six reviewer stress-tests, nine hours of wall-clock time on a MacBook Air with no GPU. Each experiment was forced by the result of the previous one. The story is not a confirmation — it is a discovery arc.

Experiment 1
Does a pre-failure signal exist in the agent's activations?
Setup
PPO + IMPALA CNN (624k params) trained 500k steps on fixed-goal CoinCollect (goal always at (6,4)). Top-K SAE (K=32, hidden 1024) trained on 100k frozen activations. Goal shifted at deployment.
Experiment
Label SAE features. Measure when goal-tracking features deactivate vs when reward degrades. Compute k across 60 episodes, 3 seeds.
Findings
k = 157.8 ± 80.3 steps. Goal features deactivated before reward degraded in all 60 episodes. 785/1024 SAE features dead. Causal graph KL max 0.0028 — noise floor only.
Open Question
Are these genuine goal features, or position-proximity detectors? The causal graph was built from cosine similarity, not intervention. Is the k = 157 signal real or a perceptual artefact?
Experiment 2
Does the causal graph work — and do the features actually track the goal?
Setup
Same frozen policy. Attempt EAP for graph edges. Retrain SAE with resampling (SAEv2: 384 hidden, 100/384 dead). Test graded shift (1–3 cell displacement). Introduce W = D^T · W_enc^T as EAP fix.
Experiment
Validate EAP vs patching. Check actual-goal-tracking correlation of features. Compare k_graph vs k_activation on graded shift. Validate W-matrix against patching.
Findings
EAP r = 0.146 (failed). W-matrix r = 0.893 (passed). Max actual-goal-tracking correlation: r = 0.005. "Goal features" were position detectors. k_graph = k_activation at every displacement (Δ = 0).
Open Question
The fixed-goal training never required a goal representation. What if goals are randomised during training? Does the pipeline work on a policy that genuinely has a goal feature?
The Deep Finding

A cleaner SAE revealed: the policy never learned where the goal is. It memorised a fixed location. This is Type B goal misgeneralization — no goal representation exists. There is nothing to monitor, graph, or correct.

Experiment 3
Does R_reason correct the agent — even with confounded features?
Setup
Same fixed-goal policy. Second PPO phase on test distribution. R_total = R_env + λ · R_reason. λ sweep {0.0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0}, 3 seeds each.
Experiment
Compare failure rate of R_reason vs baseline (λ=0) across λ values and seeds. Validate R_reason noise floor on training distribution.
Findings
λ=0: 16.7% failure. All λ > 0: 100% failure. R_reason penalised every step away from (6,4) — rewarding goal misgeneralization. Dense confounded reward destroyed policy at even λ=0.1.
Open Question
The failure was confounded features, not the mechanism. Would R_reason work with genuine goal-tracking features? Only one experiment can answer this.
The Lesson

Validate features before using them as a reward. Plain fine-tuning (λ=0) outperformed everything — because it let the policy build the goal representation it never had.

Experiment 4 — The Capstone
The full pipeline on a policy with a genuine goal representation
Setup
One change only: goal_fixed=False. Goal randomised every episode. Same IMPALA CNN, SAEv3 (0/384 dead features), W-matrix validated (r=0.89), all five invariances, all three responses.
Experiment
H1: does goal feature exist? k_graph vs k_activation at blind spot (6,5). Compare all three responses: R_reason, activation steering, targeted fine-tuning.
Findings
H1 passed: r = 0.44 (vs 0.005). k_activation = nan, k_graph = 200. R_reason: 0% failure. Steering: never triggered. Fine-tuning: repaired=True but 100% failure. Hierarchy inverted.
Open Questions → Stress-Tests
Cherry-picked? λ band? Automatable? Procgen scale? Other failure modes? Online correction? All six answered below.
The Central Discovery
Activation monitoring checks if the agent knows where the goal is. The causal graph checks if the agent is using that knowledge. Those are different questions — and they can have different answers.
k_activation = nan: goal feature active throughout, monitoring sees nothing. k_graph = 200: causal edge from feature to action broken at step 0. The agent perceived the goal and ignored it.
